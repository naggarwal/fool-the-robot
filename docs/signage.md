# Booth signage copy — "Can You Fool the Robot?"

Print-ready copy for the four signs at the booth. Nothing here needs editing on
the day. Everything is written to be read from a standing distance by an adult
walking past, or by a parent standing behind their child.

**Printing notes**

- Privacy notice: half-letter (5.5 × 8.5 in) or larger, portrait, on the front
  edge of the table where parents stand. This is the one that must never be
  hidden behind the prop basket.
- Leaderboard header: landscape, mounted above or beside the monitor, with
  space beneath it for a whiteboard or sticky notes.
- "What is this?" panel: letter portrait, on an easel or taped to the backdrop
  at adult eye height.
- Table tent: half-letter folded, sits next to the object zone.
- High contrast, dark text on white. No script fonts. Body text no smaller than
  18pt.

## 3. "What is this?" panel — for parents

> # Can You Fool the Robot?
>
> ### An AI that guesses — and shows you how sure it is.
>
> This laptop runs an image model called CLIP. When your child holds something
> up, it compares the picture against about fifty short written descriptions
> — *"a photo of a sneaker," "a photo of a banana"* — and reports which one is
> closest.
>
> That's all it does. It has no idea what a shoe *is*. It has never held one. It
> matches pictures to words, and it will always pick something, even when the
> answer isn't on its list.
>
> **So we show two numbers, not one:**
>
> **Which one is it?** &nbsp;&nbsp;The bars rank its best guesses against each
> other. There's always a winner, even when the robot is completely lost.
>
> **Does this look familiar at all?** &nbsp;&nbsp;The dial asks a different
> question — whether this looks like *anything* it has seen before. Show it
> something genuinely new and the dial drops, even while the bars still confidently
> name something.
>
> That gap between the two is the whole lesson, and it's the same one we teach
> businesses: **AI estimates. It doesn't know.** A system that can't tell you when
> it's out of its depth will be confidently wrong instead — and it will sound
> exactly as certain either way.
>
> Everything runs on this laptop. No cloud, no internet, no data collected.
>
> **ExplAIned Consulting** · AI that businesses can actually trust
